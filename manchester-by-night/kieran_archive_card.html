Case ref: 1987/B, Manchester

Blood Bond claim, disputed. Neonate (name redacted, per own request, still living) alleged forced Bonding by sire as grounds for release from Sire obligations under old Barony custom. Precedent sought: none found locally — referred to a Liverpool ruling, 1962, itself referring to something older nobody could actually produce a copy of.

Ruled, eventually: Bonding alone insufficient grounds; intent to Bond as coercion is the actual test, and near-impossible to prove after the fact unless witnessed. Neonate's petition denied. Neonate left the city within the year. Sire remained. Draw your own conclusions about how "fair" that particular precedent really was.

Filed here rather than the main court archive because Cleo asked me not to keep bringing this one up in front of Miers. Apparently it "sets a bad tone." I disagree, obviously, or I wouldn't still have the card.

Note to self: chase down whether the Liverpool ruling actually exists anywhere, or whether it's just something everyone's been citing to each other for sixty years because nobody wants to be the one who says it might not.
