Clan

Tremere

In the brick-vaulted cellar beneath Merchants' Quay, Lesley Woodrow works alone.

Upstairs, the restaurant is still finding its rhythm for the night — laughter through the floorboards, the clink of glasses, mortals paying a small fortune to be flattered. Down here the air is cold stone and old brick, and nobody comes looking without a reason Mark Floros's people would recognize on sight. Lesley has one.

He's cleared a private room's table to bare wood and marked it out in his own Vitae, drawn from a bite at his wrist, the lines dark and glossy by the second candle. Four flames at the points. A ledger open at his elbow, its margins dense with corrections on corrections — every failed attempt logged, refined, tried again.

He opens the wound fresh, lets three drops fall into the circle's centre, and speaks the words low and precise. The flames lean toward him like they're listening.

Nothing happens. Not tonight either.

He sits back and runs his tongue over the punctures at his wrist, the skin knitting closed beneath it before he's even lowered his arm. Patience. He has more time than anyone alive, and that's the thing nobody in this city seems to understand. He isn't doing this to impress the old guard — he's long past expecting their blessing. He's doing it because someone has to start rebuilding what the Pyramid's fall took from all of them, and it clearly isn't going to be the Ancillae content to sit in Miers's court taking orders from people who still look at Tremere like a liability.

He has a chantry already, technically — himself and a handful of Warlocks meeting wherever they can go unnoticed, borrowed rooms and stolen hours. It isn't much. It isn't nothing, either. Every ritual he masters alone tonight is one more thing he can eventually teach them, one more reason the handful becomes a dozen, the dozen becomes something with a name people have to respect.

He closes the ledger, snuffs the candles one by one, and climbs back toward the noise and warmth upstairs, ordinary again. Whatever he's building down here isn't finished yet. But it's his, and it's coming.
