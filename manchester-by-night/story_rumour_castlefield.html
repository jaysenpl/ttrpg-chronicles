Rumour

Castlefield, After Midnight

The Romans built here first — that's the fact everyone half-remembers and nobody quite pictures properly, not really, not until you're standing under the black iron ribs of the old railway viaduct with the fort's low stone footprint laid out beside you like a floor plan someone forgot to build a house on top of. Two thousand years is a long time for a city to keep circling back to the same few hundred metres of ground.

The Metrolink hums somewhere close, a tram pulling in, doors opening onto nobody, doors closing again. Deansgate-Castlefield station sits bright and busy just a few dozen metres off — cameras, staff, the ordinary late-night churn of a city that never fully stops moving. Nobody down here tonight is thinking about what else this ground might have seen, over that much time. Why would they?

Under the arches, though, past where the path stops being lit properly, there's a smell that doesn't belong to any of it — something faintly like tallow, like old stone that's been kept warm a very long time by something other than the weather. If you stood still long enough — nobody does — you might notice the quiet down there isn't quite the same quality of quiet as everywhere else. Not empty. Attended.

A door, unmarked, set into brickwork that looks original if you don't look too closely. It doesn't advertise itself. It's not meant to.

Somewhere below street level, further down than the Roman foundations, further down than anyone building a station or a viaduct or a canal basin ever had reason to dig, something ancient is being kept. Not hidden, exactly — hiding implies effort, implies fear of being found. This is older and stranger than that. This is a thing that simply doesn't need the surface world to know it's there, the way a heartbeat doesn't need an audience to keep going.

The tram pulls out again. Full carriage, ordinary faces, ordinary tiredness. None of them look down as they pass over the old stones.

Whatever's down there is still awake.
