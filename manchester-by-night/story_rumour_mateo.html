Rumour

The Diplomat

The cleaners have been and gone. The chairs are still up on the tables at the back, legs in the air like something dead, and the only light comes from the corridor and the streetlamps on Oxford Road.

Mateo sits at the front with a legal pad. At the top of the page, in neat capitals, it says QUESTIONS. There are four so far. There have been four for the last forty minutes.

He isn't writing. He's talking, quietly and evenly, to the empty room.

"I left him just after one. He said he had people to see, and I didn't ask who. You didn't ask him things. I came back the following night and he wasn't there. The night after that, they found him."

He stops. He tilts his head, like a man listening to a recording of himself.

"Just after two," he says. "I left just after two."

He starts again from the top, and this time it's word-perfect. The same pauses land in the same places, and there's the same small, rueful half-smile at you didn't ask him things. He's told it to the Sheriff's people. He's told it to anyone at court who asks, and to a few who didn't. By now it sounds less like a memory than a speech.

His phone lights up on the desk. It's a number he's never saved.

Anything?

He looks at it for a long moment. Then he types a reply, reads it back and deletes it. He types another, and deletes that too. Finally he sends:

Not here.

He turns the phone face down and squares it neatly against the edge of the pad. Then he picks up his pen and looks again at the first of the four questions, the one he asks every new arrival who wants the Camarilla's protection.

Who made you?

He underlines it twice.
