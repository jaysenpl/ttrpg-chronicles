Predator Type — Sandman

Marilyn Haig

The window latch gives on the second try, silent, exactly the way she's practiced it to be. She's inside and still before the curtains have finished settling back into place behind her.

The house sleeps the way most houses do — unevenly, in layers. A radiator ticking somewhere below. A phone charging on a nightstand, screen dark. She stands in the doorway of the back bedroom for longer than she strictly needs to, not out of hesitation but out of habit — the same discipline that has her double-check a locked door twice, recite a plan back to herself before executing it, trust nothing that hasn't been verified first.

The man in the bed hasn't stirred. Won't. She's chosen well tonight, the way she always does — deep sleeper, no pets, a spare room down the hall that tells her he lives alone. Nothing left to chance that could have been controlled for in advance.

She kneels at the bedside with the same unhurried precision she brings to everything, finds the vein without fumbling for it, and drinks in careful, measured pulls — enough, never more than enough, calculated down to a margin she's tested on herself more than once. He doesn't wake. Doesn't so much as change the rhythm of his breathing. She's proud of that, in the quiet, private way she's proud of most things she's disciplined herself into doing perfectly.

There's something in his blood she can't quite name — some low, settled contentment, a person whose life fits him well enough that he's stopped noticing it fits. She drinks it anyway, the way she drinks everything: thoroughly, correctly, leaving no more trace of herself than she arrived with.

She's out the window and gone before the radiator ticks again. Somewhere underneath the satisfaction of a hunt executed flawlessly, there's a harder thought she doesn't examine too closely tonight — that control, taken this precisely, this completely, is the only kind of power she's ever really trusted.
