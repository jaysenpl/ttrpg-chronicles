Clan

Malkavian

The bass doesn't stop so much as change key, floor to floor, a different pulse behind every door. Jerri likes the top level best — the one with the projections, where the light doesn't so much illuminate the crowd as dissolve it, turn two hundred dancing mortals into one long shifting animal. Easier to think here than it has any right to be. Easier to watch, too.

Jerri spots him near the second bar: a Night Forum regular, young enough that he still thinks his convictions are armour rather than a story he's telling himself. He's holding court with a couple of neonates, something about solidarity, about the Unchained looking after their own, about the Movement meaning something.

Jerri's heard some version of this speech before. Said some version of it, once, in Liverpool — meant every word, right up until the Camarilla fell and the city they'd all promised each other turned out to be exactly as hungry and small as the one they'd torn down.

"You believe that," Jerri says, sliding in beside him. Not a question.

He blinks. "Course I believe it."

"Say it again when one of them's inconvenient to protect." Jerri smiles, not unkindly. "Say it again when it costs you something."

He opens his mouth to argue, and Jerri watches the certainty flicker — just for a second, just long enough to see it isn't as solid as the speech made it sound. Good. That flicker is the only part of this Jerri actually trusts anymore.

Because here's the thing nobody at the Forum knows: Jerri isn't testing him for the Movement. Jerri stopped fully believing that speech somewhere between Liverpool and here, watched the whole thing curdle once already, felt what's left of it curdle again since. This is just what Jerri does now instead — pick at the seams of other people's certainty, and see what's underneath. See who's ready for something that isn't just another cycle of power seeking power.

The lights change again. Jerri doesn't wait to see if he has an answer.
