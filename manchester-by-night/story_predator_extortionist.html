Predator Type — Extortionist (Covert)

Kieran Cotterill

Priya's on her break, same as every Thursday, in the stairwell nobody uses because the door alarm's been broken for months and everyone who works here knows it. She doesn't look surprised to see him. She hasn't looked surprised in about three years now, not since the debts got bad enough that a stranger's quiet offer to make them disappear stopped sounding like a trap and started sounding like the only good luck she'd had all year.

He never calls it what it is. Neither does she, not out loud. It's easier for both of them if it stays unnamed — a favour, an arrangement, the kind of thing you don't examine too closely because examining it too closely would mean admitting how thoroughly you've been managed.

She rolls her sleeve up without being asked. That's new, this last year — she used to wait for him to ask. He's not sure if that's resignation or something closer to relief. Probably doesn't matter which.

He feeds carefully, precisely, taking only what he's calculated he can take without her needing to lie about feeling faint again. There's fear in it, threaded through everything else — not sharp, not fresh, just a low constant hum of someone who's stopped expecting her life to get easier. He doesn't examine that too closely either. It's simply what's there, the taste of a person who's already decided this is just how things are now.

He straightens her sleeve back down for her when he's done, a small, almost tender gesture that means nothing and costs him nothing. She'll go back to her shift. He'll go back to whatever ledger or precedent he was chasing before this. Neither of them will mention it again until next Thursday.
