Predator Type — Osiris

Robert Vasile

There's a woman at the bar who'd be easy. Vasile clocks her in about four seconds — drunk enough, alone enough, dressed like she's trying too hard to convince herself she's fine. He doesn't so much as glance twice.

Not his type. Never has been, not in any way he's ever bothered explaining to himself. What he wants is further down the room: a man in a good suit, mid-conversation, laughing at exactly the right moment, entirely at ease in a room full of people trying to impress each other. Composed. Unbothered. The kind of person who's never once needed anyone's permission to be here.

Vasile watches him for a while before approaching — not out of caution, just patience. The introduction, when it comes, is effortless. Vasile's good at this part. Twenty minutes of easy conversation, an offer of a better view from the private terrace, the man entirely unbothered by how quickly he's agreed to step outside with a stranger.

Out there, away from the windows, Vasile doesn't rush it. He likes this part too much to rush it. When he finally bites down, what comes up is exactly what he was after — still, settled, unhurried, a mind that doesn't panic even now, doesn't fight, simply accepts what's happening to it the way it's accepted every other good outcome that's ever come its way. Vasile drinks slowly, savoring the calm of it, the sheer unbothered stillness of someone who has genuinely never needed to struggle for anything.

He lets the man go a little unsteady, a little pleasantly confused, already constructing some other explanation for the lost time. Vasile straightens his jacket and feels something settle into place behind his own eyes — a little more weight behind whatever he says next, a little harder to refuse. He's noticed the pattern without needing to name it. Cause or coincidence, it hardly matters. It works.
